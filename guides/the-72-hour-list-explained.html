Guides/Planning

The 72-hour list,
explained

The Government's Prepare campaign asks every household to be able to look after itself for three days, and in October 2026 its Be Ready push asked again, louder. The list is short and sensible. Here it is, line by line, with what is already in your bag and what you still need to put in the cupboard.

The listline by line

What the Government says, and where it is

  1. Bottled water. About 2.5 to 3 litres a person a day to drink; 10 litres a day is 'most comfortable' with cooking and washing. The bag: the first day's pouches and tablets to treat more. The cupboard: the rest. See the water guide.
  2. Non-perishable food for three days, and a tin opener. The bag: two emergency ration bars with a five-year date. The cupboard: tins and dried food you already eat, rotated, and a tin opener.
  3. A battery or wind-up torch, and a battery or wind-up radio. The bag: a head torch with batteries fitted, and a wind-up and solar radio with its own torch. Nothing to buy.
  4. A portable power bank and spare batteries. The bag: the radio has a built-in USB power bank you can charge by hand or sun. Add your own phone cable and a charged power bank if you have one.
  5. A first aid kit, hand sanitiser and wet wipes. The bag: a compact first aid kit, masks and gloves, sanitiser and wipes. The cupboard: your own regular medicines, see the medication guide.
  6. Baby supplies and pet supplies. Not in the Standard Issue, which is for one adult. Pack your baby's own ready-to-feed formula so you don't need to boil water, nappies, and three days of the animal's own food. See the baby and pet guides.
The gapsa bag can't fill

What belongs in the house

  1. Water in volume: the bag is the walk, the house is the store. Three days for four people is 30 litres to drink. Keep it under the stairs with the date written on.
  2. Warmth at home: blankets, a sleeping bag per person, and a way to heat one room safely. The bag's foil and bivvy are for outside.
  3. Copies of important documents, in the bag's document pouch and in the cloud. Insurance numbers, prescriptions, the children's details.
  4. Know how to turn off the gas, the electricity and the water. Label the switches. Sign up for flood warnings and keep Emergency Alerts switched on on your phone.
The planthe list doesn't print

People, not things

  1. Two meeting points and an out-of-area contact, on a card in the bag. See the meeting point guide.
  2. Who in your street lives alone, and who checks on them. The Priority Services Register for anyone vulnerable.
  3. A child who has opened the bag before, and knows the whistle. Ten minutes, see the child's guide.
  4. A date in the diary when the clocks change: check the batteries, the water dates, the medicines, the phone numbers. Our reminder emails do the remembering.

The honest line

A go bag is not a stockpile. It is the first day, carried, and the means to make the next two safe. The cupboard is the rest, and the plan on the card is the part that matters most. Buy the bag for the walk; fill the cupboard for the wait.
