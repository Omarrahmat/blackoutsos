Our story

Built by a foster carer who kept a bag by the door.

Blackout SOS was started in London in 2026 by Omar Rahmat, a foster carer of seven years who runs supported accommodation for young people leaving care. In a house of five people and a cat, with children who had already lived through enough, the question was never whether to have a go bag. It was why the ones you could buy were so bad.

The problem

The kits on sale were a rucksack with things thrown in: a cheap torch, a foil blanket, a tin of biscuits with nuts in it, camouflage on the outside and nothing for a child, a baby or a dog. When the Government asked every household to hold three days of supplies, the answer on the shelf was a bag you'd be embarrassed to carry and couldn't find the plasters in.

The idea

One bag per person, because an emergency doesn't happen to households, it happens to people. One colour per bag, so a seven-year-old grabs the right one in the dark. Four pouches with a colour each, so the first aid is where it was last time. Food everyone can eat. A bag you'd carry on the Tube.

The rules

We don't sell fear. We point to 999 and the authorities first, we check every guide against official advice, and we don't make a claim the label can't back. Vegan and made without nut ingredients means we can show you the paperwork. Waterproof means ten minutes under a shower head.

How we make them

Designed in London. Assembled in the UK.

The bags are made to our drawings from the same TPU-laminated Cordura, taped seams and YKK AquaGuard zips as expedition packs. The contents are sourced from UK suppliers wherever one exists, checked against the claims on the box, and packed by hand in the UK into pouches with a place for everything. Every bag leaves with a rotation card, and we hold the dates so you don't have to.

The company behind the brand is Urban Carers Ltd, registered in England and Wales. If you want to talk to the person who packed your bag, write to us: it will be a short conversation, because we are a small team, and a useful one.
